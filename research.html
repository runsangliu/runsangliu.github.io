Sensor-based modeling,
monitoring, and control

ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING · QUALITY ENGINEERING

Advanced manufacturing processes increasingly rely on in-situ sensing to improve information visibility. My work develops sensor-based methodologies for image-guided monitoring and statistical process control (SPC).

In Situ layerwise inspection and multiscale monitoring

I developed an additive Gaussian Process (AGP) model to decompose standard geometric profile and spatiotemporal correlated error in layerwise imaging data, as well as compatible multivariate control charts for detecting process shifts. I further extended this framework to Wavelet Gaussian Process (WGP) that enables multiscale analysis of variance (ANOVA) of AM imaging data.

Probabilistic modeling and characterization of melt pool morphology

I developed a multi-mode probabilistic model to characterize melt-pool variability in metal AM and characterized melt-pool behavior under different process conditions.

Project support: NSF CMMI-2531899, NSF CMMI-2011289, and NIST Cooperative Agreement 70NANB19H127.

Self-organizing networks for
multiphysics modeling

COMPUTATIONAL MODELING · MANUFACTURING & HEALTHCARE

Network representations provide a compact way to describe the structure and interactions of complex physical systems. My research develops self-organizing networks for efficient simulation modeling of multiphysics dynamics.

Thermal modeling in additive manufacturing

I develop a self-organizing network approach for efficient prediction of heat accumulation and transfer in geometrically complex parts during selective laser melting (SLM) process. The network model achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0475 in predicting normalized temperature while reducing computational time by approximately 7-fold compared to a finite-element implementation.

Electrical and mechanical dynamics in human heart

My work develops a sparse network representation of heart geometry, that enables simulation modeling of the conduction and propagation of cardiac electrical signals, as well as mechanical contraction. These studies demonstrate a new pathway for cardiac simulation on a sparse network representation.

Project support: NIH R01 HL172292 and NSF CMMI-2531899.

03

Digital twinning
and model calibration

MODEL–DATA INTEGRATION · DYNAMIC SYSTEMS

Trustworthy digital twins require methodologies that align computational representations with physical observations. My work investigates the calibration of network-based models, from geometric structure to evolving physical dynamics.

Geometry calibration

I investigate how imaging data can guide the calibration of a self-organizing heart network, linking a compact representation with observed anatomy.

Calibration of electrical dynamics

I study computational methods for synchronizing cardiac electrical models with observations, extending network simulation toward digital twin operation.
